Calumny's Dominion


People cause trouble. No matter what happens, behind almost every conflict, rumor, or act of harm, there is a human choice. We often speak about kindness as if it is our default nature, yet cruelty can seem just as familiar. Whether evil is natural or learned is a question that has existed for centuries, but one thing is clear: people are capable of creating suffering just as easily as they are capable of creating good.


The world is filled with judgment. No matter who you are or what you achieve, someone will dislike you. You could have millions of people who admire you, yet a single person who hates you can still become the loudest voice. That reality never completely disappears.


In many ways, your existence in another person's mind depends entirely on their perception of you. People rarely know the complete truth about someone. Instead, they build an image from fragments, assumptions, rumors, or whatever they choose to believe. Once that image exists, changing it becomes incredibly difficult. You can explain yourself a thousand times, present evidence, defend your character, or correct misinformation, yet many people will continue believing what they decided to believe from the beginning.


Belief is powerful. It shapes people's realities more than facts often do. Whether it comes from ideology, personal bias, social media, or rumor, people tend to protect the narratives that make sense to them. Your perspective may never matter if it conflicts with the version of reality they have already accepted.


This is why so many people spend their lives trying to validate themselves. Online, this is especially visible. Many women, particularly in male-dominated communities, feel pressured to constantly prove their worth, authenticity, or even their right to participate. But this pressure is not limited to one group. Countless people exhaust themselves trying to satisfy strangers who were never interested in understanding them in the first place.


The truth is that almost anything can be fabricated. Stories can be invented. Images can be manipulated. Documents can be forged. Even evidence that appears convincing can be manufactured. Because of this, demanding endless proof of someone's innocence or identity is rarely a practical solution. It often becomes a trap where no amount of evidence is ever considered enough.


When trust disappears, paranoia grows. Every explanation is questioned. Every denial becomes suspicious. Every attempt to defend oneself is interpreted as another lie. It becomes an endless cycle where nothing is ever settled because some people simply do not want to be convinced.


Disliking someone is not the problem. Everyone has people they do not get along with, and there is nothing wrong with choosing not to interact with them. If you do not like someone, simply leave them alone. There is no obligation to become friends with everyone.


The problem begins when dislike turns into hostility. Having a negative opinion, hearing an unverified rumor, or making assumptions about someone does not give anyone the right to destroy that person's reputation. Whether you know them personally or not, they are still another human being whose life can be affected by your words.


Some people cannot be convinced, and recognizing that is important. There comes a point where proving yourself is no longer productive because the other person has already decided who you are. At that stage, no amount of explanation will change their mind.


You do not have to spend your life validating your existence for strangers. You are not required to earn the right to exist through endless proof, endless arguments, or endless approval.


You exist because you exist—not because someone else believes you do.


Live for yourself, not for the people who are determined to misunderstand you.
